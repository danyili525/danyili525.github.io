<p>
  I am a PhD Candidate in Preventive Medicine (Health Behavior Research) at the Keck School of Medicine, University of Southern California, and a pre-doctoral fellow funded by the California Tobacco-Related Disease Research Program (TRDRP). My dissertation project aims at examining the impact of local and statewide tobacco control policies (e.g., flavored tobacco sales restrictions) on youth tobacco use behaviors, using quasi-experimental designs and causal inference models. My training is multidisciplinary, spanning social and developmental psychology, epidemiology, biostatistics, and public policy. Before joining USC, I earned a Master of Public Health in Biostatistics and Epidemiology, a Master of Social Policy at Washington University in St. Louis, as well as a Master of Public Finance and Policy at Renmin University of China.
</p>

<p>
   My research interests focus on how public health policies and services, prevention interventions, built environments, and other systemic social determinants of health shape health behaviors and outcomes among youth. I use quantitative, qualitative methods, and systematic reivew and meta-analysis in my studies. Specifically, my work applies: Quasi-experimental designs to study natural experiments such as tobacco-control policies and natural disasters; Observational cross-sectional and longitudinal designs, regression-based and machine-learning methods, using large population-level survey data; Clustered randomized controlled trial designs to evaluate school-based prevention interventions; Qualitative methods, including thematic analysis of social media content and in-depth interviews with health providers in substance use treatment centers and schools. My work seeks to identify risk and protective factors for youth substance use, addictive behaviors, obesity-related behaviors and outcomes.
  
</p>
